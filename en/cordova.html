We diagnose Cordova/Ionic apps whose iOS updates are blocked

Since April 28, 2026, apps uploaded to App Store Connect must be built with Xcode 26 and the iOS 26 SDK. Older Cordova/Ionic build environments fall prey to this requirement first. We will only look at the publicly available store information and explain what is blocked. It's free for the first 10 companies, and we'll respond within 2 business days.

The free diagnosis only lays out what is blocked. How to fix it, and the fix itself, are paid. No source code, signing key, or store account required.

Three things to diagnose

Apple SDK Minimum Requirements Applicable from 2026-04-28

App Store Connect uploads require Xcode 26 or higher and the iOS 26 SDK. An old cordova-ios or an older build pipeline can block update submission altogether. Apps already installed are not removed from users' devices right away.

Google Play Target API 2026-08-31, extension application until 11/1

New apps and updates must target Android 16 (API 36) or higher. There are exceptions for each form factor. If an existing app targets below Android 15 (API 35), its availability to new users on devices running a newer OS than the app's target is restricted. Not all users and all devices will be blocked. Depending on the app, you may be able to request an extension until 2026-11-01 in the Play Console.

Firebase Dynamic Links Service ended on 2025-08-25

According to the Firebase official FAQ, the service has been terminated. page.link addresses that return 404 are most likely dead. If a link has been left unattended for nearly a year, it may not have been bringing in any traffic to begin with. So, in the diagnosis, we look separately at whether there are dead links and how much traffic they bring in.

Cordova·Ionic apps are often tied to native plugins and outdated CLIs, making it difficult to keep up with the latest Xcode and Play targets alone. However, this does not mean that Cordova is officially archived. Each app has different circumstances.

Apps that fit and apps that don't fit

  • B2C app or external app that requires new installation, membership registration, or recruitment
  • App that requires iOS hotfix but has broken build
  • Cordova/Ionic app the original developer no longer maintains
  • Teams with no or only one in-house mobile manager

Apps used only by field staff with no new installs may be little affected by Play policy. If so, the diagnosis says so plainly.

How to check directly

You can check iOS recent uploads first, then Play target, and deep link if applicable. Deep links can be confirmed in 30 seconds without a developer, but don't assume a loss of sales just by looking at 404.

  • iOS upload. Check the App Store or in-house build history to see if there have been any successful uploads or deployments since 2026-04-28. If there are no records of success, your upload may be blocked. This is the item with the greatest impact.
  • Cordova or not. Unzip the APK and check whether assets/www/cordova.js or cordova_plugins.js exists. If it does, it's most likely Cordova-based.
  • Play target API. aapt dump badging app.apk prints targetSdkVersion; check that value. If it is below 35, availability to new users on devices with a newer OS may be restricted after 8/31. Apps used only by staff who already have them installed may be less affected.
  • Deep linking. From the app links on your homepage, QR codes, text messages and emails, paste any page.link address into your browser. If it's 404, the link is dead. You need to check separately whether it has brought in any traffic.
  • Period of neglect. View the last update date for the store. The older it gets, the more work there may be in your build environment. Don't judge by date alone.

If it is difficult to check yourself or you do not have an in-house representative, apply for a free diagnosis. If nothing applies, we will tell you so.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is the diagnosis free?

It costs very little because we only look at public sources. The first 10 companies are free, to build references, after that there is a fee. If nothing applies, we will tell you so. Detailed fix instructions are provided only at the paid stage.

Do I need to give the source code or signing key?

Not required for diagnosis. Repository access may be required during the implementation stage, but the store signing key and Play/App Store account can be designed to remain in the customer's possession. Basically, we recommend delivering the code and building it in the customer's build pipeline.

Are apps used only by field employees also affected by Play policies?

If there are few new installs and the app is used only on devices that already have it, the impact of the new-user restriction may be small. The bigger problem with these apps is that they cannot ship iOS hotfixes and security patches. When diagnosing, we will ask whether you distribute through an in-house MDM or get new installs through the store, and we will tell you accordingly.

Can’t I just apply for an extension?

Extending Play only buys time. According to official information, you may be able to apply until 2026-11-01. The requirements do not go away and go up every year. There is no extension period for iOS SDK requirements.

If I move to Capacitor, do I have to rewrite my web code?

Most of it is reused as is. What we change is the native runtime and plugins. Having outdated Angular or obsolete plugins increases man-hours. A list of problematic items will be included in the diagnosis, and instructions on how to fix them will be provided during the paid design stage.

Can I hand the signing key to an outside studio?

Not recommended. We recommend a contract that divides the scope into stages and leaves signing and submission to the store under the client's authority.

Can’t the deep link be replaced with a new link?

Links you send out from now on can simply be changed. Printed materials, texts, and URLs of external sites that have already been distributed cannot be retrieved, so server redirection is required. If there is no traffic, there may be no reason to spend money on restoration.

Can this be solved if I use Branch or AppsFlyer?

Both services are tools for creating new links. They do not automatically revive old addresses that are already dead. Setting up a redirect and replacing the link creation part of the app code are separate tasks.

Can Japanese companies also request it?

We can handle the work in Japanese. You can also send inquiry emails in Japanese.
